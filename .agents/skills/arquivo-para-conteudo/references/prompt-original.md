# 07 · Turn your archive into new content

**Why it works:** if you have been publishing for years, your archive already holds your next quarter of content. The job is mapping the themes you keep returning to, then remaking the strongest pieces in your voice: one piece becomes a thread, a newsletter section, and three shorts, siblings instead of strangers.

**Prompt:**

```
Turn my archive into my next quarter of content.

My archive: my videos and posts about [marketing], plus my [newsletters]. Use Apify to collect what is public, and I will hand you exports for the rest.

Process:
1. Collect everything and build the index: every piece, its topic, its format, and roughly how it performed where the numbers are visible.
2. Map the themes that keep coming back. For each theme: how often I return to it, which pieces carried it best, and what I used to say that I no longer say.
3. Pick the ten strongest pieces: the ones whose core idea still holds. Say why each earned the slot.
4. Remake each of the ten as a content family in my voice: a thread, a newsletter section, and three short scripts, all from the same core idea so they land as siblings, not strangers.
5. Deliver the calendar: which family ships which week, and what each piece links back to.

Rules: my voice, my positions. If an old piece contradicts what I say now, flag it, do not modernize it silently. Nothing invented: every new piece traces back to something I actually made.
```

**Fill in:** the topic, the newsletter source. Apify is one way to collect public posts; swap in your own exports if you have them.

**Pro tip:** read the theme map even if you remake nothing. Seeing what you keep returning to tells you what your brand is actually about.

**What you keep:** a content engine fed by your own archive. The theme map keeps paying after the ten families ship.
